# Alan Watts: ideas map for Fieldy

Purpose: a map of the ideas in Uri's Alan Watts books that Fieldy can be built on, written for the session that designs the section. It is a map of ideas, in our own words. It contains no quotations and no passages from the books, and nothing here may be presented to a user as something Watts said.

Rules that follow from that:
- Every piece of content in the app is written fresh, in Fieldy's own voice, in Hebrew and English directly (neither is a translation of the other).
- Anything shown to a user is labeled "inspired by Alan Watts". Never put words in his mouth, never use a quotation mark around something he did not write.
- Do not reuse the books' own examples, jokes or analogies. Use examples from Fieldy's world: sites, contractors, stuck pumps, the drive to Cauayan, a full task list, a quiet garden.
- The books are not in the repo and must not be added to it.

## What was read, and how deeply

Read for structure and key chapters, not line by line (about 640,000 words in total):
- The Book on the Taboo Against Knowing Who You Are: structure, the opening of each chapter, and the six "social fictions" list in the chapter "The world is your body".
- The Meaning of Happiness: all eight chapter openings (war in the soul, the answer of religion, the way of acceptance, the return of the gods, the vicious circle, the one in the many, the great liberation, the love of life).
- The Way of Zen: contents and preface (Tao, Buddhism, Zen; "Empty and marvelous", "Sitting quietly, doing nothing", za-zen and the koan, Zen in the arts).
- The Essential Alan Watts (excerpt): contents, and the "Ego" chapter in detail.
- Alan Watts Lectures and Essays (transcripts), Philosophies of Asia (two files): contents and openings only.
- The Early Writings (1931 to 1938, young Watts) and Here and Now (essays about Watts by other scholars): not mined for content. Here and Now is commentary by others, useful only as background.

A deeper pass per idea is possible by giving the design session the books directly. If that is done, it must still write everything in its own words.

## The ideas

Each idea: the thought, the book it comes from, and where it can live in Fieldy.

1. **Trying hard works against itself.** Forcing attention, courage or willpower creates strain and makes the thing worse. Relaxed attention sees and hears more. (Essential, "Ego"; Way of Zen, effortless action.)
   - Fieldy: Quick calm, a hard-task prompt ("soft focus instead of grinding"), meeting prep.

2. **The separate "I" is a useful story, not a fact.** We behave as if a self sits inside the skin, apart from the world. Seen differently, you are the whole organism and its surroundings. (Taboo, "The world is your body"; Essential, "Ego".)
   - Fieldy: sites, people and the garden as one system. A person is part of a field of relationships, not a manager standing above it.

3. **Six social fictions** (from the Taboo): the world as separate things, things as made of one stuff, the individual as a thing with an ego inside, opposites as true enemies, death as evil and life as a war against it, and humanity as the species that should control nature. Each is a convention that can be held lightly.
   - Fieldy: one idea per week, framed as a small "what if" about work, not a doctrine.

4. **Opposites need each other.** Light and dark, solid and space, success and stuck. Neither can win for good.
   - Fieldy: stuck sites, "good day, bad day", the mood model. A hard day is part of the same wave.

5. **War in the soul.** Trying to get rid of feelings by fighting them keeps the fight alive. (Happiness, ch. 1.)
   - Fieldy: mood tips that name the feeling and let it be, before any action. This must never replace real support for someone in real distress.

6. **Acceptance is not escape and not surrender.** Accepting what is happening is the starting point for acting well. (Happiness, ch. 3.)
   - Fieldy: the Now/Today/Later review, stuck-task triage, "what is true right now".

7. **The vicious circle of the spiritual quest.** Wanting to be free, calm or enlightened becomes another thing to chase. (Happiness, ch. 5.)
   - Fieldy: protects the app from becoming a self-improvement treadmill. Streaks stay forbidden (the app already has none). A "you are allowed to do nothing" card.

8. **The seeker is what is sought.** Happiness comes less from chasing it than from noticing who is looking. (Happiness, ch. 7.)
   - Fieldy: the end-of-day reflection ("what already worked today"), not "what to fix".

9. **The way of the Tao: working with the grain.** Effortless action, water, spontaneity. (Way of Zen, "Philosophy of the Tao"; "Sitting quietly, doing nothing"; Zen in the arts.)
   - Fieldy: already in the Tao tab. Link each practical idea to a Tao chapter.

10. **Work as play, time as now.** Doing a thing for the doing, and the present as the only place anything happens. Plans serve life, not the other way around. (Essential, "Work as play" and "Time".)
    - Fieldy: the closing of a task, the fieldwork day, the drive. A moment to enjoy the thing you are doing before the next.

11. **Life as a drama and a game.** You can play your role well and still see it as a role. (Essential, "The drama of it all"; Philosophies of Asia, mythology.)
    - Fieldy: Loki's lightness. The area manager's role, the contractor's role, handled with humor.

12. **Cooperating with nature, not conquering it.** The unity of organism and environment as a practical stance. (Lectures and Essays.)
    - Fieldy: the garden, weather and farm work, solar systems as a collaboration with the sun.

## Loki in the spirit of Watts

Uri wants Loki to carry these ideas as though a playful guide in the spirit of Watts were presenting them, and the ideas to spread through the whole app. Design guidance:

- Loki stays Loki. He never claims to be Alan Watts and is never named as him. The section says "inspired by Alan Watts".
- The spirit to borrow, not the words: amused, warm, gently irreverent, fond of paradox, never preachy, comfortable with not knowing, laughs at taking oneself too seriously (including Uri's own seriousness about work).
- Style rules: short, concrete, one idea at a time. A question is stronger than a teaching. No jargon from Sanskrit, Chinese or Japanese unless it is explained in a line, and not as decoration.
- Humor must never land on real pain. When the app detects a heavy mood (overwhelmed, anxious, lonely, sad, angry), Loki gets quieter and plainer, and philosophy waits.
- Never use these ideas to talk someone out of a decision that deserves seriousness (safety on site, money, a contractor's wellbeing). Acceptance does not mean inaction.

## Where the ideas can spread (touchpoints)

Keep each touch small and optional. A setting "Loki's philosophy: light, more, off" lets Uri set the intensity.

- Home: one short Loki line a day that carries one idea, replacing or joining the quote card.
- Closing a task: a short play/enjoy line sometimes, never a lecture.
- Mood tips: a gentler version of the existing lines per mood, tied to ideas 1, 5 and 6.
- Morning briefing: the single "one thing that matters" framed with idea 10.
- Quick calm: idea 1 as the introduction to the 60 seconds.
- Review and triage: idea 6.
- Leadership tab: ideas 2, 4 and 11 for working with people.
- Garden and Loki's story: the opposites and cooperation with nature (ideas 4 and 12), built into the story chapters.
- Tao tab: a dedicated Watts section with the full idea cards, each linked to its Tao chapter.
- Notifications: only on explicit opt-in, never the philosophy by default.
